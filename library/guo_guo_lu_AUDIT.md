# Guo, Guo and Lu 2025 (Piatetski-Shapiro PNT): certification audit note

Instance: `guo_guo_lu.json` (coverage mode). Source: L. Guo, V. Z. Guo, L. Lu, "The Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem," arXiv:2505.10391v2 (8 December 2025). Certified claim: a coverage-gap witness exists iff `gamma <= 8886224/10318869`, matching Theorem 1.1, `pi_c(x) = x^{1/c}/log x + O(x^{1/c}/log^2 x)` for `1 < c < 10318869/8886224`. Run: `python3 certify.py guo_guo_lu.json` passes 60/60 checks (15 slices, base toolkit plus three ablations). The verdict was independently reproduced by a 1-D interval scan written directly from the instance, agreeing with the engine at all 60 checks and at every witness location.

This is the current record for the asymptotic form, so the instance supersedes `rs_pns.json` as the record exemplar. The improvement over Rivat-Sargos is small enough to be worth stating exactly: `2426/2817 = 0.86119986` against `8886224/10318869 = 0.86116259`, a gap of `3.73e-5` in gamma and `5.02e-5` in c. The slice `gamma = 2426/2817` sits between the new threshold and 13/15, and the certificate is required to report coverage there.

## Linearity verdict (the census question)

Verified, with the same boundary as Heath-Brown 1983. Once the exponent pair is fixed at the paper's choice, every condition in sections 5.2 through 5.4 is rational-affine in the block coordinate `mu = log_x M` and the parameter gamma. Jointly in `(kappa, lambda, gamma)` the system is bilinear, because Theorem 1.2's exponents multiply gamma through the substitution `H = x^{1-gamma}`. Optimization over the exponent-pair hull is therefore a design choice outside the certifiable linear layer.

## Encoding summary

Design substitutions, all the paper's own: `alpha := 1-gamma` and `l := 5` in Lemma 3.3 (Rivat-Sargos), `H := x^{1-gamma+eps}` for the Vaaler truncation height, and the exponent pair `(kappa, lambda) = (10769/351096, 609317/702192)`. The paper cites this as Theorem 20 of arXiv:2501.16779. In the ANTEDB blueprint's own numbering it is Theorem 5.22 ("New exponent pairs"), it is derived in `derived.py` as `prove_exponent_pair(frac(10769,351096), frac(609317,702192))`, and it appears as vertex n = 8 of Table 5.2. It is not an A/B-process word: the blueprint proves it from the beta(alpha) bounds tabulated in its Table 5.1 together with Lemma 4.10 and Lemma 5.3, so unlike Heath-Brown's `BABA^2BA^2B(0,1)` it enters this certificate as an imported external input rather than a re-derived one. Note also that the hull has moved since Tao-Trudgian-Yang. Blueprint Theorem 5.23 records two Cushing (2025) pairs as vertices n = 5 and n = 6, so Table 5.2 rather than TTY's H is the current set of known pairs.

The three pieces are Lemma 3.3's three obligations at `alpha = 1-gamma`, plus two empty-toolkit gate pieces for its hypothesis `1/2^l <= alpha <= 1/6` at `l = 5`. Two transcription decisions are worth flagging.

First, the type I′ piece carries Lemma 3.3's own window `mu in [2/3, 2gamma-1]` and not the paper's narrowed window `x^{5gamma-4} << N << x^{1/3}`. The narrowing is a consequence rather than an input. A type I′ sum is a special case of a type II sum, so Lemma 3.1 covers the part of the window with `mu > 5-5gamma`, and the engine derives the effective burden `mu in [2/3, min(2gamma-1, 5-5gamma)]` on its own by carrying both tools on that piece.

Second, the two constructed-edge cases here resolve in opposite directions, which makes this instance the clean illustration of why sandwich item 3 asks whether an edge is *constructed* rather than whether it merely coincides. Lemma 3.1's lower edge `mu >= gamma` carries no fail atom, because `alpha := 1-gamma` is chosen precisely to seat the decomposition's window floor on that edge, and reinstating the atom plants a spurious boundary witness at `mu = gamma` for every gamma. Term 6 of Theorem 1.2 is adequate exactly when `N >= H^2 = x^{2alpha}`, which coincides with the type I′ window's own lower-N edge for the same underlying reason, and its fail atom is nevertheless retained. That coincidence is a single boundary point which the type II tool covers whenever `gamma > 6/7`, so no spurious witness arises and the genuine facet 6/7 survives.

## Findings

**Finding 1 (threshold reproduced exactly, and the facet is again identity geometry).** The three printed exponents of E1 and the published threshold all re-derive on the nose from the first term of Theorem 1.2 under the substitution `(X, H, M, N) -> (x, M, H, N)`, with H at full strength `x^{1-gamma}` and the witness at `mu = 2/3`. That location is the point `N = x^{1/3}`, the ceiling Lemma 3.3 inherits from the Heath-Brown identity's requirement `V^3 >= 32x`. So as in Heath-Brown 1983, the binding constraint is the geometry of the identity rather than the interior of any estimate. The full facet map, in order: `19/39 < 1/2 < 2/3 < 10778789/15996564 < 3/4 < 4386825/5762948 < 5101094/6533739 < 15/19 < 18403303/22530303 < 5/6 < 6/7 < 8886224/10318869 < 13/15 < 31/32`. The runner-up is 6/7, where three independent facets coincide: the type II containment `2-2gamma <= 5gamma-4`, the type I bound `1-gamma/2 <= 3gamma-2`, and term 6 of Theorem 1.2.

**Finding 2 (three printed errors, all harmless).** E2's printed x-exponent 1.1743 is a typo for the exact `9288253/5332188 = 1.741922`. It does not propagate, since the paper's own derived condition for E2 uses the correct value. E4's row is garbled twice over. Its inequality direction is printed as an upper bound on M when the printed M-exponent `-0.212` forces a lower bound, and its gamma-condition is printed as `0.838` when the exact value is `18403303/22530303 = 0.816824`, which is not a rounding of anything derivable from the paper's own decimals. Both errors are slack against the threshold and neither affects Theorem 1.1. The remaining printed conditions agree with the exact values to rounding.

**Finding 3 (seven of the nine printed conditions are vacuous, not merely dominated).** The type I′ obligation window `mu in [2/3, 2gamma-1]` is empty below `gamma = 5/6`, and the paper's analysis window `[2/3, 5-5gamma]` is empty above `gamma = 13/15`. The entire type I′ burden therefore lives on `gamma in [5/6, 13/15]`. Of the paper's nine printed conditions, only E1 at `8886224/10318869` and E6 at `6/7` have facets inside that band. The other seven have facets at or below `18403303/22530303 = 0.8168 < 5/6`, where the window they are conditions on does not exist. This is a stronger statement than domination, and it is the direct analogue of the Lemma 9 slackness finding in the Heath-Brown instance. Separately, Theorem 1.2 has twelve terms and section 5.4 prints nine. The three unprinted terms are options 10 through 12 in the instance and yield `5/6`, `2/3` and `1/2`, so dropping them was justified, though the first of those coincides with the `l = 5` gate and is not trivially negligible.

**Finding 4 (the ablation reproduces Liu-Rivat exactly, and the citation is not load-bearing).** Removing Theorem 1.2 entirely leaves the type I′ window covered only where Lemma 3.1 reaches, and the certified threshold degrades to exactly `13/15`, which is Liu-Rivat's `c < 15/13`. The type I′ bound is thus load-bearing precisely on `gamma in [5/6, 13/15]` and does nothing outside it. The converse also holds and is worth recording: section 5.2 restricts to `gamma < 13/15` and cites Liu-Rivat above, but the certificate shows the paper's own Lemma 3.3, Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 5.1 already cover `[13/15, 31/32]` with no type I′ input at all, because the type I′ obligation is absorbed by the type II estimate there. The citation is a convenience rather than a necessity.

**Finding 5 (the pair choice is optimal among the four new pairs but not over the known hull).** Blueprint Theorem 5.22 lists four new pairs, and computing the full twelve-term threshold for each shows the paper picked the best of them: `8886224/10318869` against `18374/21333`, `3580570/4156599` and `8030/9321`. Over the full hull of Table 5.2 the choice is not optimal. Vertex n = 7, the pair `(9/217, 1461/1736) = A(18/199, 593/796)`, gives the strictly better threshold `7300/8477 = 0.86115371`, that is `c < 8477/7300 = 1.16123288` against the published `c < 10318869/8886224 = 1.16122090`. The variant instance `guo_guo_lu_A7.json` is the same transcription with only the pair changed and it certifies that threshold at 16/16, reporting coverage at the paper's own threshold `gamma = 8886224/10318869`. The improvement is `8.9e-6` in gamma and `1.2e-5` in c, which is a fifth-decimal correction to a fifth-decimal improvement, and it requires no new analysis at all. Two caveats. Vertex n = 7 has been available since Tao-Trudgian-Yang, so this is a missed optimization rather than a consequence of later work. And the optimum is a vertex optimum: E1's threshold is linear-fractional in `(kappa, lambda)`, so its minimum over the hull is attained at a vertex, and E1 was the binding term at every one of 4000 sampled points on the hull, with none beating `7300/8477`.

E1 binds for every pair tested, including Bourgain's `(13/84, 55/84)` and Heath-Brown's `(11/53, 33/53)`, so the architecture's binding facet is stable under the pair and only its location moves. The dependence is not monotone in kappa. The trivial pair `(0,1)` returns `13/15`, which is worse than the record, so there is a genuine interior optimum. As in the Heath-Brown instance the certified band is two-sided. The upper edge is `31/32`, from Lemma 3.3's admissibility hypothesis `alpha >= 1/2^l` at `l = 5`, and it is retired by taking `l >= 6`, which is a change of design substitution rather than of the linear layer.

## Mutation results

`mutate.py` on the final instance: 22 of 104 mutants killed (`guo_guo_lu.mutation.tsv`). All four perturbations are killed on exactly the two atoms that define the threshold, namely term 1 of Theorem 1.2 and the type I′ window's `mu >= 2/3` ceiling, and all four are killed on Lemma 3.1's atom `mu <= 5-5gamma`, which the `no_typeIprime_bound` ablation makes observable through its 13/15 threshold. Five further groups are half killed, and the pattern is uniform: both loosening directions die and both tightening directions survive. This is what should happen for an atom whose facet lies below the threshold, since loosening lifts that facet above the threshold and manufactures a witness where coverage is expected, while tightening only deepens a region that already carries a witness. The half-killed groups are terms 6 and 10 of Theorem 1.2, Lemma 5.1's atom, the type I window's ceiling, and the `l = 5` upper gate.

The 82 survivors are all accounted for. The dummy `mu in [0, 1]` rows on the two gate pieces are structural and carry no content. Nine of the twelve Theorem 1.2 options have facets at `3/4` or below and cannot be lifted to the threshold by a `1/50` perturbation. The type I′ window's ceiling `mu <= 2gamma-1` is unobservable in principle here, because for `gamma > 6/7` the effective ceiling is always `5-5gamma` from the type II tool and for `gamma < 6/7` a witness exists regardless. The most interesting survivor class is the type II window's two region rows, which survive for a quantitative reason worth recording: a `1/50` RHS perturbation moves that piece's facet by at most `1/350 = 0.00286`, while the gap from the runner-up facet `6/7` up to the threshold is `0.00402`. The mutation magnitude is simply too small to cross it. By contrast the upper gate at `31/32` is observable only because the top slice `19/20` sits `0.0125` below it, inside `1/50` reach. Both observations are properties of the harness's fixed perturbation size against this instance's facet spacing, not defects in the transcription.
